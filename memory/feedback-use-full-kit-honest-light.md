Read `references/gear.md` and use what's there: if they own more than one light, use more than one. Gel
concepts: one colour at a time when asked. **Every plan lights ONE named frame** and sits under that frame on
the board. Every stand and the camera stand on real ground: mark where nothing can stand (air past a railing,
water, drops, traffic) and never put a light there; check each light is out of frame at the stated lens and
distance. The AI frames obey the same physics: a gel colours what it hits, the shadow is where that colour is
missing and attaches at the feet; beams only in haze. An independent reviewer (fresh agent with the plan,
frames and real photos) checks every plan before publishing. When recreating a reference, read its light first
(highlights, shadow direction, rays, camera height) and copy that direction. Honest beats "two flashes + gel"
when the place doesn't allow it; say why.
**Why:** "I have more than one flash already"; "the light composition doesn't make sense... take the wall into
account"; a rim light placed past a bridge railing: "HOW AM I SUPPOSED TO PUT THE LIGHT BEHIND HER? IN THE
AIR?"; "the light diagrams aren't even attached to any specific image"; red shadows on white tiles.
**How to apply:** every lighting section; `lighting-diagram` skill.
